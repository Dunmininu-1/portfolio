Retail Sales & Returns Analysis

PostgreSQL • Power BI • Power Query • DAX

Project Overview

This end-to-end retail analytics project explores 49,222 transaction records to understand sales performance, customer spending patterns, order outcomes and product returns.

I used PostgreSQL to validate the dataset and investigate business questions, then used Power Query and Power BI to prepare the data, develop analytical measures and build interactive dashboards.

The project demonstrates my approach to data quality, exploratory analysis, KPI reporting and communicating findings that can support business decisions.

Business Questions

  • How much sales revenue was generated?

PostgreSQL Analysis

I used PostgreSQL to validate the retail transaction dataset and investigate business questions before building the Power BI dashboards. The analysis focused on order performance, customer spending patterns, data quality, and category-level issues.

Order Status Performance

I analyzed order performance by status to compare completed, cancelled and returned orders including their contribution to total sales and their percentage of total orders.

Customer Tenure & Spending

I analyzed customer tenure to understand how customer longevity relates to order frequency and average customer spending. Longer-tenured customers showed higher average order activity and spending.

Data Quality Investigation

I investigated unusual transaction values to identify potential data-quality issues before analysis. The review found negative and zero-value transactions, which were examined rather than removed without investigation.

  • 18 transactions with negative quantity
  • 19 transactions with negative sales
  • 19 transactions with negative order value
  • 5 transactions with zero quantity, sales, and order value

Further investigation showed that the negative-sales transactions included records with a Completed status, highlighting the importance of validating unusual values before making business decisions.

Category Issue Rate

I compared order outcomes across product categories to identify categories with higher levels of cancelled and returned orders. This helped highlight areas where product or order issues may require further investigation.

Key Findings

  • Order completion: Completed orders represented 91.98% of all orders, generating approximately ₦3.16 million in sales.
  • Returns: Returned orders accounted for 3.06% of total orders, with returned sales of approximately ₦101,815.
  • Product returns: Tablets recorded the highest returned sales among the highlighted products at approximately ₦10,439, followed by mechanical keyboards at ₦10,053.
  • Payment methods: Gateway payments accounted for approximately ₦52,591 in returned sales, making them the largest contributor among the payment methods analyzed.
  • Data quality: Negative and zero transaction values required investigation before interpreting the results. This reinforced the importance of validating unusual records before making business decisions.

Business Recommendations

  • Investigate unusual transactions: Review negative and zero-value transactions, particularly records marked as Completed, to confirm whether they represent legitimate adjustments or require correction.
  • Monitor product returns: Investigate the reasons for returns involving tablets, mechanical keyboards and office chairs before deciding whether product descriptions, quality checks or customer support processes need improvement.
  • Review payment-related returns: Examine gateway payment transactions associated with returns to determine whether the high returned-sales value reflects payment volume, transaction value or a specific issue.
  • Track order outcomes: Monitor completion, cancellation and return rates over time to identify meaningful changes and support operational improvements.

These recommendations are proposed next steps based on the observed patterns. Further investigation would be needed before attributing causes or implementing changes.
